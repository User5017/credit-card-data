2. Task 5 prep: FDIC BankFind API

Two scouts, both read-only, both through carddash.http.make_session(). Scratch JSON and the field dictionary are under C:\Users\samwe\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\scout-fdic\.

Access

  • No key. Base https://api.fdic.gov/banks/, endpoints /institutions, /financials, /history. Docs at /banks/docs/, dictionaries risview_properties.yaml, institution_properties.yaml, history_properties.yaml.
  • Envelope: meta.total, meta.index.name (vintage stamp; risview_20260819185831 already holds 2026-06-30), data[].data, totals. Row id is <CERT>_<REPDTE>.
  • Filters are Elasticsearch query strings: CERT:(628 OR 3510) AND REPDTE:[20250101 TO 20261231], NOT BKCLASS:(NC OR OI). REPDTE is YYYYMMDD on financials but MM/DD/YYYY on institutions. Field names must be uppercase.
  • limit max 10,000 (500 if more than 250 fields). One request per cert with limit=10000 returns the full history (170 quarters from 1984-03-31 for cert 628). A 33-cert pull with 16 fields came back as 4,588 rows in 4.9 s.
  • Rate-limit headers X-Ratelimit-Limit: 120; window length unverified; never hit 429 in about 80 calls.
  • Silent failures. A not-yet-published REPDTE returns 200 with zero rows. A wrongly formatted date returns 200 with zero rows. An unknown field name in fields= is dropped without error. The fetcher must assert every expected key is present and that each cert returned rows.
  • Industry aggregation works: agg_by=REPDTE&agg_sum_fields=LNCRCD,...&limit=0. Exclude BKCLASS NC (noninsured) and OI (insured foreign branches) to match the QBP's 4,238 institutions and $26.46tn assets exactly.

CFPB complaints trends endpoint

  • https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/search/api/v1/trends (no trailing slash) answers the pipeline User-Agent in 0.4 to 6 s. Database 17.6m complaints; product "Credit card" 340,648 across 178 monthly buckets from 2011-12, continuous.
  • Grammar: lens=overview alone works; lens=product|issue|company needs focus= or sub_lens=; sub_lens_depth must be at least 5; sub-lens buckets are capped at 10 whatever the depth; trend_interval=month.
  • Which aggregation to read: dateRangeBuckets ignores every filter except the date window (it is the whole database). dateRangeBrush honors product, company and issue filters and ignores the date window: that is the monthly series. With lens=product&focus=Credit card&sub_lens=company, the top-10 companies each carry a monthly trend_period plus sum_other_doc_count.
  • Per-issuer beyond the top 10: one request per company with company=<exact name>&lens=overview. Company names are as filed; Discover will not roll into Capital One without a crosswalk.
  • Issue taxonomy is windowed ("Fees or interest" exists only from 2023-08); the current and prior months are incomplete (2026-08 has 6,695 so far). Drop the current month, mark the prior one provisional.
  • No release page states monthly numbers; a golden would come from the annual Consumer Response report product totals.

4. Page critique and task 7 notes

Read-only review of render.py, the template, the views and the rendered docs/index.html against the rendering rules in CLAUDE.md. Ranked by how much each would mislead a reader.

  1. Offered vs paid compares the wrong thing. "Offered" is the median of each product's maximum APR (sql/tccp_facts.sql line 33), against the average rate paid. The 5 to 7 point spread is mostly definitional. The spread also runs two quarters past the last offered reading (the as-of join carries H2 2025 into 2026 Q2), and its unit caption says percent when it is percentage points. Fix: use the tier medians already in facts (superprime, 620_719, le619) as a band against the G.19 paid line; stop or dash the spread past the last offered period.
  2. SLOOS is dated to the survey quarter, so today the page reports a latest period of 2026 Q3, which has not ended, and the step is drawn into the future. The July survey asks about the previous three months. Fix: a per-series period offset in series.csv applied in the FRED fetcher; refuse to call a future period "latest".
  3. A chart card cannot go red, and its "pulled" date is the last attempt, not the last success (loader.py line 258 sets pulled_at before fetching). Fix: show "data as of" from the plotted rows' pulled_at and a status badge per card.
  4. "What changed" filters on the newest run in revisions.csv, not the latest run (render.py lines 319 to 320). After the first revision ever, every later run keeps listing it. Also: rel_change is inf when the old value is 0; new periods are never listed; revisions.csv is neither copied nor linked. The file does not exist yet, so the block is right by accident.
  5. Cross-source footers merge sources and cadences alphabetically and take the max latest period, so the reader cannot tell which line is semiannual. One footer line per series when sources or cadences differ.
  6. Method section credits only the Fed via FRED. CFPB, Philadelphia Fed and NY Fed are loaded but uncredited. Generate the list from SOURCE_LABELS.
  7. Population is not visible at a glance. Scope notes are collapsed; source_url is in the payload but never rendered. Add a short population column to series.csv and print it on the caption line.
  8. Light-mode contrast is weak (orange 3.1:1, green 2.7:1 against the card surface; orange and green are the deuteranopia pair; muted text at 3.5:1). A fourth series silently reuses blue.
  9. Step charts lose their first reading: uPlot's stepped(align:-1) draws point i from x[i-1] to x[i], so the first point is a zero-width tick and every since cut hides one period. Query from one period earlier and clamp the x range.
  10. spanGaps: true on every series would bridge any real gap. Not triggered today. Set it only for foreign-grid nulls on mixed-cadence charts.

Lower: the method text says nothing is interpolated while three charts are lines; SA/NSA is not in legend labels; the health-strip parenthetical picks an arbitrary series; mixed-cadence tables print ISO dates; the zero-based y rule has no per-chart override (needed before score percentiles get charted); the selection overlay is invisible in dark mode.

The reference for any cross-source performance chart: the NY Fed's Liberty Street post of 2026-08-11, "How Distressed Are Consumers? Reconciling Diverging Credit Card Delinquency Measures". Charged-off balances leave lender books but stay in bureau data, and the share still reported a year later rose from about 40 percent in 2004 to about 80 percent in 2024. That is why the HHDC 90+ share sits far above the Philly Fed and call-report rates, and it belongs in a footnote.

What this project does that none of them do: joins the official card sources with a scope note per chart; an offered-vs-paid chart from public inputs; a revision log for sources that rewrite their history every quarter; a health strip with staleness, restatement detection and golden checks; one keyless CSV of everything, daily.

Worth copying: per-chart CSV with a stable URL and a "last N periods not final" flag (CFPB); separate data-vintage and publish timestamps and a schema file (dfri); benchmark rows under performance charts such as the 2015 to 2019 mean, cycle peak and pandemic low (americandefault.org); the weekly Bankrate APR from FRED to bracket the semiannual TCCP step; the stock-versus-flow distinction stated on the delinquency chart (NY Fed).
